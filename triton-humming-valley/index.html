Half of it stays empty.

Thirty-two villas is a deliberate number. Half the site is left as open ground — planted, walkable, and kept that way. What you buy here is as much the space between the houses as the house.

The land sits below the summit at Kuduvathi, in Bangalore North. The 9th-century Bhoga Nandeeshwara temple stands at the foot of the same hill; the cloud inversion that gives the ridge its reputation is most reliable between November and February.

Why this stretch of road, and why now

The airport corridor

Bengaluru's growth has been running north for a decade. The land between Hebbal and Devanahalli carries the city's newest infrastructure, and the ridge sits just beyond its edge — close to the airport, outside the sprawl.

Supply that cannot grow

Ridge-facing land near Nandi is finite and building on it is constrained. Thirty-two villas is the whole project; there is no phase two on this site.

It works as a second home

An hour from north Bengaluru makes this usable on an ordinary weekend, not just a holiday — which is what separates a home you visit from one you keep meaning to.

It is finished

Possession is October 2026. You are buying a house you can walk through, on land you can stand on, rather than a plan and a completion date — and you carry none of the delay risk that comes with buying early.

Can I walk through a completed villa?

Yes. The project reaches possession in October 2026, so a viewing walks a finished villa rather than a show unit or a plan. Early morning slots are worth taking between November and February, when the cloud sits in the valley.

The project is approved by leading lenders. Our team can put you in touch with the relevant bank relationship manager.
