# Stakeholder Reporting

Leadership does not want a dump of platform metrics. They want: are we on track, why or why not, what are we doing about it, and what do you need from them.

## Rules

- Lead with the answer. First line is the verdict against target.
- Business metrics first (qualified leads, visits, bookings, revenue, cost per booking). Platform metrics (CTR, CPM) only as supporting evidence, and only when they explain something.
- Every number has a comparison: vs target, vs last period, vs same period last year.
- Explain changes in cause and effect, one line each.
- Bad news early and plainly, with the fix. Never bury it.
- Separate facts from forecasts. Label forecasts with a range.
- Keep it to one page or 3 to 5 slides. Put detail in an appendix.
- Match the reader: CEO and founders want revenue and risk. Sales heads want lead volume, quality and follow up. Finance wants spend vs plan, CAC and payback. Brand teams want reach and message consistency.

## Weekly update template (email or Slack)

```
Subject: Paid media weekly | [dates] | [On track / At risk / Off track]

Bottom line: [1 to 2 sentences with the key number vs target]

Numbers (this week vs last week vs target)
- Spend: X (plan Y)
- Leads: X | Qualified: X (Z%)
- Cost per qualified lead: X (target Y)
- Site visits / meetings: X | Bookings: X

What moved and why
- [Driver 1 with number]
- [Driver 2 with number]

What we are doing
- [Action, owner, date]

Need from you
- [Decision, approval, or input from sales / creative / product]
```

## Monthly review structure (slides)

1. **Scorecard:** targets vs actuals for the month and quarter to date. Traffic light status.
2. **Funnel:** spend → leads → qualified → visits → bookings, with conversion rates and change vs last month. Shows where the leak is.
3. **What worked and what did not:** top 3 wins and top 3 problems, each with evidence.
4. **Channel mix and budget:** spend and cost per qualified lead by channel, with proposed allocation for next month.
5. **Next month plan and asks:** tests planned, budget request, dependencies.
6. Appendix: campaign level tables, creative examples, methodology notes (attribution window, data source).

## Handling tough questions

| Question | Good answer shape |
|---|---|
| "Why did CPL go up?" | Decompose into CPM, CTR, CVR. Name the main driver with the number. Say what you are doing and when you will know if it worked |
| "Are these leads any good?" | Show CRM qualification and visit rates by source. If you do not have it, say so and propose the fix |
| "Can we get more leads for the same money?" | Show the marginal cost curve. Explain the trade between volume and quality |
| "What would happen if we doubled budget?" | Scenario range with diminishing returns assumption stated |
| "Is Meta or Google better?" | Depends on role: Google captures intent, Meta creates it. Compare on cost per qualified lead and assisted conversions, and recommend a test |

## Writing style

- Short sentences. Active voice. No jargon without a one line explanation.
- Numbers formatted consistently with currency and thousands separators.
- Avoid em dashes and long comma chains. Use full stops.
- No hedging words stacked together. State confidence once, clearly.

## Delivery options

If connectors are available: draft the email in Gmail (draft only, never send without confirmation), save a doc to Google Drive, or build slides with the `slides` or `pptx` skill and charts with the `dataviz` skill.
