Star colour is a thermometer

Experiment
Kutush, imagine this

Heat an iron rod in a flame. First it glows dull red, then orange, then yellow-white. Stars follow the same rule: the colour tells the temperature. Red stars are the "coolest" (still about 3,000 °C!) and blue stars are the hottest.

The H-R diagram: a family photo of stars

Experiment
Kutush, imagine this

Plot every star by how hot it is and how bright it is, and they don't scatter randomly. Most sit on a diagonal band (the main sequence), where they spend their adult lives. Swollen giants float above it, and tiny white dwarfs sink below. Pick a star to find it.

Stargazing tonight: a starter kit

Go dark

Get away from street lights if you can, and switch off your phone screen. Your eyes need about 20 minutes to fully adapt to the dark.

Use red light

Cover a torch with red cellophane. Red light does not ruin your night vision, which is why observatories use it.

Measure with your hand

Held at arm's length, your little finger covers about 1°, your fist about 10°. The Big Dipper is about 25° long.

Planets don't twinkle

A bright "star" shining steadily near the path of the Sun and Moon is probably a planet: Venus, Jupiter, Mars or Saturn.

Special relativity
Kutush, imagine this

You fly to a star and back at almost the speed of light. When you land, your classmates are much older than you. This is not science fiction: fast-moving clocks really tick slower. Choose a destination and a speed.

Shrink anything into a black hole

Every mass has a Schwarzschild radius, r = 2GM/c². Squeeze it smaller than that and it becomes a black hole.

Time near the edge

Hover near a black hole while your friend waits far away. How much time passes for them while one hour passes for you?

Simple non-spinning black hole. Near a small black hole, tides would stretch you like spaghetti first. Near a supermassive one like Sagittarius A*, you could cross the horizon without feeling it.

Mission 5 · Classical mechanics

Newton's lab

In 1687 Newton imagined a cannon on a very tall mountain. Fire it fast enough and the ball falls around Earth forever. Try it with real numbers.

The cannon sits 200 km up, above almost all the air, so we ignore drag. At this height a circular orbit needs about 7.8 km/s and escape needs about 11.0 km/s.

Kepler's secret: equal areas

Experiment

Your weight across the Solar System

Mass or weight?

Your mass (kg) is how much stuff you are made of. It never changes. Your weight is the force of gravity on you, W = mg, measured in newtons. A bathroom scale secretly measures force and divides by Earth's g.

Mission 12 · Your path to ISRO

The real road to ISRO

Checked September 2026
  1. Now, Class 11 and 12Physics, Chemistry, Maths

    For IIST you need at least 75% aggregate in PCM in Class 12 (65% for SC, ST and PwD candidates). Understand concepts, not just marks: IIST and ISRO both test real understanding.

  2. After Class 12JEE Main, then JEE Advanced

    IIST (Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology, Thiruvananthapuram, run by the Department of Space) admits B.Tech students through JEE Advanced ranks, with its own registration.

  3. 4 yearsB.Tech at IIST, or engineering anywhere

    IIST offers Aerospace Engineering, Avionics and Engineering Physics. ISRO recruits IIST graduates on merit, subject to vacancies and grades. Any good engineering college is also a path.

  4. After graduatingISRO Scientist/Engineer 'SC'

    Through ISRO's Centralised Recruitment Board (ICRB): a BE/B.Tech with at least 65% (CGPA 6.84/10), a written test, then an interview. Age limit 28 (relaxations for reserved categories).

  5. Other doorsScience and research

    A BSc/MSc in physics can lead to research at ISRO centres and Department of Space labs like PRL Ahmedabad. College students can apply for ISRO internships. YUVIKA is ISRO's programme for Class 9 students, so tell younger friends!

Admission and recruitment rules change. Always confirm on iist.ac.in and isro.gov.in before applying.

NCERT first, then deeper

Derive, don't memorise
